Part I Pattern-Breaking Ideas: The Counterintuitive Forces That Power Revolutionary Products

“A WAVE OF CLARITY Introducing Inflection Theory”

  1. A pattern-breaking idea is a specific product or service based on an insight
  2. An inflection is an event that creates the potential for radical change in how people think, feel, and act.
  3. The future is already here; it’s just not evenly distributed.
  4. An insight is a nonobvious truth about how one or more inflections can be harnessed to radically change human capacities and behaviors.
  5. An idea is an attempt to conceive of some specific product or service, based on insights.

HARNESSING INFLECTIONS

  1. They didn’t offer something unique enough that people desperately wanted
  2. Why do they miss the wave? The answer is that many start-up founders begin by looking for trends in the here and now, or for big markets with white space and pain points. As a result, they fail to discover inflections that can help them transcend the status quo.

TIMING IS EVERYTHING

  1. You can think of it this way: Every breakthrough will happen. The question is when, not if.
  2. Be careful about ideas that sound good but are not grounded in powerful inflections.
  3. Do not rush to reject ideas that sound crazy at first; they may be great ideas that tap into powerful inflections.
  4. Be skeptical when someone says it’s been tried before and didn’t work.
  5. Stress-test your ideas by considering their underlying inflections in sufficient detail.

DIFFERENT IS… DIFFERENT

  1. Developing a compelling insight is one of the most important parts of the job description for a breakthrough founder
  2. You have to be right and non-consensus.(It's realy hard to reach the hard right)
  3. For unconventional success, start-ups need to create something unique that people are desperate for.
  4. inflections and insights are mutual multipliers—inflections create the conditions for radical empowerment, and insights create the conditions for radical differentiation
  5. A start-up’s advantage, by contrast, depends on breaking people’s patterns of behavior—on replacing established patterns with new ones.
  6. Why do so many fail to get there? A key reason is that many start-up founders begin by looking for underserved markets and unmet customer needs. They then feel compelled to immediately create a product that meets market demands and customer needs in the here and now. But, in doing so, founders frequently skip the crucial step of determining if their idea embodies an inflection and an insight. As a result, they come up with an idea that does not offer compelling enough value (because it lacks an inflection with sufficient empowering capabilities) or enough differentiation (because it does not rely on a unique insight). As a result, they often miss out on the opportunity to pursue an idea with pattern-breaking potential.
  7. think beyond the obvious

STRESS-TESTING YOUR INSIGHTS

  1. A pattern-breaking idea must transcend fleeting attention. It should offer a transformative empowerment that stands the test of time, replacing existing norms.
  2. But ultimately, the biggest failure mode I see with insights is the same as the most common failure mode with inflections: a lack of specificity.
  3. Discovering compelling insights is where the true artistry of breakthrough founders lies

“LIVING IN THE FUTURE Where You Will Find Your Next Insight

  1. Breakthrough insights come from living in the future and tinkering directly with what’s new about it—not by having passing ideas about it from a distant vantage point.

GET OUT OF THE PRESENT Avoiding a Common Entrepreneur Mistake

“BEFORE YOU CROSS THE RUBICON The Implementation Stress Test

  1. The implementation stress test helps you by creating a prototype with simulated features tailored to resonate strongly with a select group of early adopters. Once the right people see the right implementation of your prototype, they will be desperate to use the real version.
  2. Implementation prototype: A focused deliverable that helps you engage potential early believers to identify:
    What is the most important benefit?
    Who are the most desperate customers?”
  3. What can we uniquely offer that people are desperate for?
  4. The only way to truly fail in a start-up is to lose your time, especially after the point when you know the start-up was never worthy of it. The only thing you can never get back is that time.

  1. Surprises are so important to understand because you are pursuing a non-consensus idea for a future that is mostly unknown or at least ambiguous.
  2. offering customers too many benefits is a problem
  3. Instead, focus on a select group who deeply connect with your future insights. I refer to these individuals as “co-conspirators” because they share your beliefs and see the potential.

YOUR FIRST TRUE BELIEVERS Attracting the Perfect Customers and Investors

  1. Early customers are not simply people who buy what you’re selling. They’re motivated by belief—belief in the world that you’ve articulated in your story
  2. The original Tesla Roadster wasn’t just a minimum viable product; it was a minimum viable future
  3. It’s important for you to focus on only the customers who can accelerate your path to building what’s missing in the future.
  4. A raw start-up has two things: the start-up team and the insight.
  5. Everyone involved—the team, early customers, and investors—share the right kind of crazy.
  6. If people aren’t moving to the future the founder inhabits, it’s up to the founder to spend more time with those who are ready to move, or to be more effective in giving the right people reasons to move, or some combination of both.

LAUNCHING YOUR MOVEMENT Overturning the Status Quo

  1. A movement is a group of people with a shared belief in moving together toward a different future.
  2. Airbnb’s founders reframed the choices about lodging in a way that makes this similarity seem like a problem rather than a benefit.
  3. Your story must villainize the status quo by describing its problems, and by sharply contrasting between the world governed by the status quo and a world freed from its dominance
  4. it is an equally important second step to avoid the temptation to be liked by normal people who still believe in the status quo.

TELLING YOUR STORY The Hero Isn’t You

  1. It works because our brains are wired for stories. When you hear a powerful story, your heartbeat might increase and your body temperature may rise. It’s a physical experience, and it’s involuntary
  2. Steps
    • STEP 1: APPEAL TO A HIGHER PURPOSE
    • STEP 2: ATTACK THE STATUS QUO（Repositioning incumbent strengths as weaknesses is a potent opportunity）
    • STEP 3: CREATE A HERO’S NARRATIVE (AND REMEMBER, THE HERO ISN’T YOU)
    • If your story is going to move people, it can’t be about you. It must be about the early customers, employees, and investors you’re trying to inspire, and the higher purpose you want them to embrace.
    • STEP 4: FORCE A CHOICE, NOT A COMPARISON(Founders fall into a comparison trap whenever they compete on features or price or any aspect of “betterness,”)
    • STEP 5: FIND YOUR BREAKTHROUGH LANGUAGE
  3. A start-up wins when it breaks the pattern and introduces a fresh concept that reshapes people’s perceptions and sets its own standards
